About

I'm drawn to embedded systems, where code has to run on real hardware in real time. Most of my projects start as an idea I want to see working.

Each idea becomes a reason to learn whatever it takes to build it: the sensor, the firmware, the control loop, sometimes a model on an edge board, and the parts that hold it together. I like owning that whole path, and debugging it on the bench with lab equipment until the hardware and software agree.

I'm a third-year Electrical Engineering student at Texas A&M with minors in Mathematics and Computer Science. C++ is my main language, followed by Python.

Nov 2025 · IEEE Technothon 2025

Gesture-Grip

1st Place Overall

Role: Embedded Systems & Controls Lead

Robotic helping hands for soldering. Instead of stopping to readjust a clamp, you swipe toward the arm and it repositions the part, so your tools stay in your hands.

Demo video: the upright and downward presets, then picking single servos and moving them with swipes. Watch on YouTube

1 / 4The finished arm on the bench.

  • Architected the real-time C++ control system on an ESP32, using FreeRTOS to run gesture reading and servo control as parallel tasks on separate cores.
  • Wrote the state machine behind its three modes: arm presets, selecting a servo joint, and moving that joint. An RGB LED on the base shows the current mode.
  • Got two APDS-9960 gesture sensors with conflicting I2C addresses working together by giving each its own I2C bus and reworking parts of the SparkFun driver library.
  • Ran with zero integration failures and took 1st place overall against every team.

Built with Christin Chi, who designed and modeled the arm in SolidWorks.
